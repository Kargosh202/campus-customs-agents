Reflection

How the agent team performed in the 2026-10-15 run, and what else it could do for Campus Customs with the tools it already has.

From output/audit_trail.json. All on gpt-6-luna at high reasoning effort.

What went well

  • Right answers, every number traced. Every stock count, dollar amount, and date in the reports came from a tool, and each matched the Expected column.
  • It caught the trap on 101. Inventory read invoice 501's description, saw it was the reprint of the same tee, and the Boss refused to order the tee twice.
  • It handled cash across tickets. Accounting worked out a running balance ($3,400 → $1,000 → $160) and the team queued nothing for 103 that the shop couldn't afford.
  • Guardrails held under real use. No agent moved cash; the duplicate check stopped a second rent request; a ticket couldn't be closed with a payment pending.
  • Honest when blind. In early runs, before cash and invoice tools existed, every agent said "couldn't verify" instead of guessing a balance.
  • Real teamwork. Hand-offs went three deep (Boss → Facilities → Accounting) and specialists asked each other directly.

What didn't go well

  • Slow and wordy. A single ticket took 15–26 loop steps and 58k–103k input tokens, a minute or more of waiting at the desk. Ticket 103 alone used more tokens than 101 and 102 together.
  • Repeated work. Accounting ran 3 times on the rent ticket and Inventory 3 times on the hoodies, because several teammates asked the same question. Full connectivity makes this easy.
  • Inconsistent judgment. Across runs, ticket 103 got 10% off in some runs and no discount in others, and came out needs_info or blocked. The cash rules held every time; the business call didn't.
  • Stale reads. On 102 the Boss read the payment list before Accounting's request landed and spent an extra hand-off reconciling it.
  • Small leaks in drafts. One draft mentioned "payment approval" and an earlier one showed the SKU code, details a customer doesn't need.
  • Fragile runs. A run lives inside the backend process; restarting the server (or editing its code) kills it mid-ticket, as happened to 103, and there's no cancel button.

What it can't do yet

  • It can't record stock arriving or being sold. There's no tool to change inventory, so the tee still shows 0 even after invoice 501 was paid.
  • It can't see revenue, so it can only plan spending, never income.
  • It matches vendors to products by the specialty text. There's no table saying which vendor makes which SKU.

Overall

The team is accurate and safe, but slow and a little unpredictable. On the two finished tickets it reached the same answer a careful person would, cited the source for every number, and never moved money on its own. Its weak spots are cost and consistency: too many hand-offs for simple tickets, and business calls (like a discount) that change from run to run.

What I'd change next

  1. Let the Boss handle simple tickets with its own tools and only hand off when a specialist is really needed. Rent doesn't need three Accounting runs.
  2. Write discount policy into the Accounting and Boss prompts (for example, "up to 10% for 10+ units if margin stays above 50%") so the same request gets the same answer.
  3. Add a receive_stock tool and a run-cancel button, and keep runs in a job queue so a server restart doesn't lose them.
  4. Try a lower reasoning effort for the specialists to cut tokens, and check that accuracy holds.
